Relevance of UI/UX for Non-Profits and the Social Impact Sector

Exhibit 1: Your NGO or CSR is passionate about a cause. You pitch your people-first programme, go through the usuals right from pitching, due diligence, documentation, finalising budget, approvals, and start working on the project to make it a success. Realising the reach and quantum of the initiative, the requirement for a website, mobile application, and grant management tool springs up, and it is managed somehow. The series of challenges begin because UI/UX was not considered an important aspect while forming the said resources.

The website has all the information, but it was a struggle to figure out the story, programme details, mission and vision, and most importantly the contact page. No proper navigation menu, cluttered content, poorly done infographics and data, haphazard order of elements, and long loading time were common for both the mobile app and the grant management tool.

The donors, funders, project implementation team, and field workers find the app and the grant management tool difficult to access. It fails to serve the purpose; instead of making the process lucid, it became time-consuming. And this is where UI/UX comes into the picture. Social impact organisations and non-profits need profound, to-the-point, simplified UI/UX for all their technical products.

UI/UX reads as User Interface / User Experience, and in layman's terms it is the interior design for your application or website. It helps your target audience gain a certain experience and reach their desired information through the right placement of content, layout, fonts, and style on each page, starting from the navigation bar.

Whether you are a CSR or funding agency disbursing and tracking grants on grant management tools like mGrant, or a non-profit using custom-built apps and data collection tools like mForm, it works wonders when your audience engages with your initiative through tech, and optimised UI/UX design is the simplest way to it. A good UI/UX will help retain old supporters and acquire new ones. It is always better to achieve the best UI/UX in the first go by investing a little more time, resources, and effort.

Understanding user needs

To create impactful websites and mobile applications, it is essential to first understand the needs and preferences of the target users. Thorough user research enables UX designers to gather insights into the challenges faced by individuals, communities, and organisations in the social sector. This knowledge forms the foundation for designing intuitive, user-friendly interfaces that address specific pain points. Are all the pages dynamic and mobile-friendly? Is the load speed good, or does it need optimisation?

Simplifying complex processes

Think from the perspective of a new user who has no idea what your organisation is: the key initiatives, mission and vision, values, donate and volunteer pages, leadership. On visiting your mobile app or website, will they be able to access this information easily? Donation or grant management, due diligence, volunteer coordination, and resource allocation are the most common complex processes for social sector organisations to simplify. UX designers can break these down into smaller, manageable steps with clear design elements: well-structured forms, content hierarchy, logical navigation, and visual cues.

Promoting accessibility and inclusivity

Accessibility and inclusivity are of paramount importance for social impact institutions. UX should ensure the interface accommodates users with diverse abilities, devices, internet connectivity, and locations, through scalable fonts, alternative text for images, keyboard accessibility, and disability support. Multilingual support and cultural nuance expand a product's reach to a broader audience.

Empathetic visual storytelling

Visual storytelling is a powerful tool to reach people. Aesthetics are important, but it is equally essential to prioritise functionality. Compelling visuals such as photographs and videos evoke emotion and create empathy; success stories, the impact of donations, and beneficiaries' journeys inspire users and stakeholders to engage more deeply. Clear calls to action and adherence to accessibility guidelines are unsaid rules.

Iterative design and continuous improvement

Flexibility and adaptability are the foremost requirements for an up-to-date UI/UX. An iterative design process allows continuous improvement based on user feedback and evolving needs. Regular A/B testing, diverse user personas and interviews, and data analysis provide valuable insights to refine the experience over time.

UI/UX analytics

Behaviour data helps in understanding what design changes improve UX. Common metrics include session duration, pages per session, return traffic, and submission rates for donations, volunteering, and newsletter signups. Analytics tools like Google Analytics, Mixpanel, Adobe Analytics, Smartlook, and Hotjar help track page performance, interaction, time spent, and user journeys.

The need for positive change is ever-present in the social impact sector, and UX design plays a crucial role in creating impactful mobile applications, dashboards, and websites. By understanding user needs, simplifying complex processes, promoting accessibility, using visual storytelling, and embracing iterative design, UI/UX can bridge gaps and empower individuals and organisations to make a tangible difference in their communities.
