Low Code & No Code: New Change Catalysts

The two most common approaches to app development have been to purchase ready-made apps from a third-party vendor or to build and customise them from the ground up with skilled developers and coders. We are now witnessing a rapid rise and advancement of technologies of low-code/no-code development alternatives that provide users throughout the organisation with the power of developing applications.

A low-code or no-code development platform enables people to build software applications without coding or with very little coding; a popular alternative to traditional software development for people with little technical expertise who intend to create full-fledged applications.

What role does low-code play in social sector organisations?

Social sector organisations very often work on limited funding, but as technology becomes a necessity in itself, more and more organisations want to integrate app or web-based solutions into their programmes. With very limited budgets, it is not just the cost of hiring specialised engineers that can impact an organisation, but also the costs of maintenance and future upgrades of the systems. Hence tech for non-profits needs to be both affordable and user-friendly as well.

What makes no-code so revolutionary for the social sector?

  1. If an organisation lacks the resources to hire a software developer or outsource to a development house, no-code platforms put innovation into the hands of every employee. Most no-code platforms provide a user interface designer that enables users to assemble pre-configured components to build a blog or software quickly.
  2. Simplifying your development process allows you to utilise your developers better across the stack, decreasing the time it takes to build and the number of staff needed to do it. Fewer people and less time mean less cost.
  3. Social sector organisations often need solutions in less time as they have to address problems continuously; hence, no-code can be a time-efficient solution.

Where the social sector can use these

  1. Back office: HRMS, websites, payroll systems, emailers, campaigns, and more.
  2. MEAL: creating systems for Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning.
  3. Donor and grant management: document management, compliance management, and related workflows.
  4. Outreach: chatbots, IVR, SMS, and other channels.

Popular no-code platforms to begin your journey with

  1. Building websites: Super, TypeDream
  2. Mobile applications: AppSheet, Glide
  3. Chatbots: Glific, Tidio
  4. Forms for surveys: mForm, TypeForm

At Dhwani we are trying to make technology more accessible to social sector organisations. Dhwani's mForm is a no-code platform where social sector organisations can create forms to collect data in the field, from farmer data management to hygiene behaviour data to tracking the progress of Self Help Groups. Using mForm makes programme planning and monitoring more accessible at the ground level by leveraging technology.
